Use this page when a problem stalls or when you need to review. Start by saying what you are missing. The clue suggests a first move, and the work still lies in checking whether the move fits your problem.

Combine moves

  • Unreachable state. Identify the legal moves, choose a candidate invariant, and verify it for each move. Compare its start and target values.
  • Optimal answer. Construct a candidate, derive a bound for every allowed choice, and check whether the candidate attains it.
  • Suspected pattern. Test small cases and edge cases, state an exact claim, and try to prove it. Change the claim if the evidence demands it.
  • Uncertain prediction. Write down the model's assumptions, estimate a range, and name an observation that could separate competing explanations.

Pick a study path

  • Starting from scratch. Follow the chapter order through Part I, then work through Part II. Do each part's checkpoint before moving ahead.
  • Preparing for contest problems. After Part I, focus on Chapters 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 17, and 24. Mix old and new problems without labeling the expected method.
  • Applying math to data or computing. After Part I, review Chapters 7, 12, 15, 21, and 22. Work through Part IV, then build and test a model for a question you can measure.
  • Starting an investigation. After Part III, try Chapters 31–35 in order. Keep a log with one question, your evidence, your strongest claim, and the next test.
