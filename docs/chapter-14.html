Chapter 14 / Part II

Split Problems and Choose a Standard Form

On this page

  • Start here
  • Worked example
  • Try it
  • Check your work

Start here

To count socks in a drawer, you could separate them by color. This works when every sock lands in one pile. Decomposition breaks a problem into manageable parts and makes you check whether they cover what you started with.

Worked example

Fractions such as 2/62/6, 3/93/9, and 1/31/3 represent the same number. Dividing numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor gives 1/31/3 each time. With a positive denominator, lowest terms give a canonical form, a standard representative for each rational number.

Two duties come with a standard form. Show that every allowed object can be put in the form. Then show that two allowed descriptions of the same object lead to the same result. A convenient-looking description alone leaves the second duty open.

Try it

  • Practice. Count the integers from 1 to 30 that are divisible by 2 or 3. Give cases that do not overlap.
  • Stretch. Show that a positive rational number has just one lowest-terms fraction with a positive denominator. You may use the fact that if bb divides adad and a,ba,b have no common factor greater than 1, then bb divides dd.

Check your work

Practice. Count all 15 multiples of 2, then the five odd multiples of 3. That gives 20.

Stretch. From a/b=c/da/b=c/d get ad=bcad=bc. Since a,ba,b are coprime, bb divides dd. By the same argument dd divides bb. Their positivity forces b=db=d, and then a=ca=c.
