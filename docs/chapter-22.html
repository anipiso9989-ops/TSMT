Estimate Growth With Bounds

On this page

Start here

An exact value can be expensive to find. Two simple bounds can still tell you how the value grows when the input gets large. Asymptotic reasoning compares growth at large input sizes.

Worked example

Let Hk=1+1/2+⋯+1/kH_k=1+1/2+\cdots+1/k. At k=8k=8, group the terms after 1 in blocks with indices 2, then 3 through 4, then 5 through 8. Each block after 1 adds between 1/21/2 and 1. For example, the last block has four terms, each at least 1/81/8 and at most 1/41/4.

For k=2mk=2^m there are mm such blocks. Block jj has 2j−12^{j-1} terms, with denominators greater than 2j−12^{j-1} and at most 2j2^j. Thus

1+m/2≤H2m≤1+m.1+m/2\le H_{2^m}\le1+m.

Doubling the cutoff adds only one block. Both bounds show logarithmic growth, because mm is the number of doublings needed to reach 2m2^m. Compare an arbitrary cutoff with the powers of two on either side.

Try it

  • Practice. Use the bounds above to put H32H_{32} in an interval.
  • Stretch. Prove that 1+1/4+1/9+⋯+1/n2≤21+1/4+1/9+\cdots+1/n^2\le2 for every positive integer nn. Compare 1/k21/k^2 with 1/(k(k−1))1/(k(k-1)) when k≥2k\ge2 and look for cancellation.
