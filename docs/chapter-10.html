Prove a Pattern by Induction

On this page

Start here

Imagine a row of numbered tiles. You know tile 1 can fall and that falling tile kk knocks over tile k+1k+1. Together those facts cover every tile with a positive integer label. Induction uses that same chain of implications.

Worked example

Show that the sum of the first nn odd positive integers equals n2n^2. At n=1n=1 the sum is 1. Assume it equals k2k^2 after kk terms. The next odd number is 2k+12k+1, so the sum after the next term is

k2+2k+1=(k+1)2.k^2+2k+1=(k+1)^2.

The first calculation is the base case. The second is the inductive step, which proves the next case from the current one. Make sure the step starts where the base case leaves off.

Try it

  • Practice. Prove that 2n≥n+12^n\ge n+1 for every integer n≥0n\ge0.
  • Stretch. Prove every integer n≥2n\ge2 is either prime or a product of primes. Use strong induction, where the step can use all earlier cases.

Check your work

Practice. 20=12^0=1. If 2k≥k+12^k\ge k+1, then 2k+1≥2(k+1)≥k+22^{k+1}\ge2(k+1)\ge k+2 for k≥0k\ge0.
